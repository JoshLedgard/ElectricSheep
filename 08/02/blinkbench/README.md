# Blinkbench — An 8-Second Interface Recall Lab

*Daily Surprise · 2026-08-02*

## What it is

Blinkbench is a self-contained product-research instrument for seeing which parts of an interface survive a brief encounter. Choose one of three wholly fictional app screens, look at it for five, eight, or twelve seconds, then paint the regions that stayed in your mind after the screen disappears. A draggable reveal places that freehand afterimage back over the original.

There is deliberately no score, grade, benchmark, or prescribed reading. The visible shape of attention is the artifact. You can repeat the experiment with another specimen and export the original-plus-recall trace as a real SVG.

Everything runs locally in the open browser tab. There are no accounts, uploads, analytics, network requests, external assets, packages, saved browser data, or server.

## Privacy-safe inspiration

The generic inspiration was the challenge of keeping the important surfaces of fast-moving work coherent—and the curiosity of discovering what remains visible after detail falls away. That broad interaction-design theme became a harmless recall lab. Blinkbench contains no real people, organizations, products, repositories, projects, accounts, conversations, messages, locations, or datasets.

## How to open

Open `index.html` in any modern browser. No installation, server, account, or network connection is required.

## Controls

1. Choose **Pulse desk**, **Orchard log**, or **Tide radio**. All are invented interface specimens.
2. Select a 5, 8, or 12 second exposure and press **Begin observation**.
3. When the screen vanishes, paint remembered attention regions with a finger, pen, or pointer. Fine, Natural, and Broad brushes change the trace size.
4. Press **Reveal afterimage**, then drag the white comparison handle sideways.
5. Use **Export SVG** to keep a two-panel visual receipt, or **Try another specimen** to repeat.

The page supports touch and pointer input, responsive phone-sized layouts, large controls, and reduced-motion preferences.

## Why it was built

Many interface tools inspect exact pixels or reduce a design to a metric. Blinkbench instead makes the user's imperfect visual residue tangible without claiming it is correct. It is a tiny, playable prototype of a qualitative research method—and an example of a browser artifact that produces a useful object rather than just displaying a theme.

## Format choice

- **Lane:** `startup_growth_experiment` — presented as a polished future-artifact and believable product-research app.
- **Mechanic:** timed observation → direct freehand recall painting on a blank phone surface → draggable visual reveal against the original → real SVG export. It is deterministic, tactile, non-scoring, and immediately playable with three built-in fictional specimens.

This lane differs from the last three surprises' calm creative ritual, practical micro-tool, and game/puzzle lanes. Its timed visual exposure and freehand afterimage mechanic differs from linked kinetic balancing, form completion/replay, and layered excavation.
