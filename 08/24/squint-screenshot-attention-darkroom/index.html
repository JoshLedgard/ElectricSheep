What each bath is forread, don't score
This is a perception aid, not an accessibility compliance test. Squinting at a picture tells you what pulls the eye first — it does not measure contrast ratios, text size, focus order, reading order, or anything a real accessibility audit checks. Treat what you see here as a conversation starter about visual hierarchy, then verify with proper tooling and, above all, with people.
How the darkroom works (and where your image goes)

Every pass runs on offscreen <canvas> elements in this tab. Thumbnail distance downsamples to a small buffer and scales it back up with smoothing. Grayscale and contrast isolation ride the native canvas filter. Squint blur pads the plate with clamped edge pixels first so the border doesn't bleed to black. Peripheral falloff builds a second, heavier layer and masks the sharp one over it with a radial gradient centred on your aperture.

There is no server, no network request, no analytics, no storage, and no persistence of any kind. Reload the page and whatever you loaded is gone. The only thing that ever leaves is the PNG you deliberately save.
