# Memory Moth Workshop

## What it is
A tiny, self-contained teaching lab you can play with one thumb in about 2–3
minutes. You furnish five glowing **memory shelves** — Tone, Next tiny action,
Why it matters, Constraint, and Follow-up — by tapping cue cards that flutter
into place. Then three little moth visitors pass through the workshop, each with
a simple need, and you watch which remembered cue each one retrieves and carries
back as a concrete, helpful little line.

It's a spatial matching toy, not a quiz. There are no scores, grades, meters, or
right answers — just a workshop you arrange and moths that show you what "keeping
just enough" actually buys you. Tap a lit shelf to retune its note, send each
moth as many times as you like, then copy a small **memory recipe** to keep.

## Privacy-safe inspiration
The recent week's energy was about queues, handoffs, recurring automation, and
the quiet art of making small systems *remember just enough context to behave
helpfully* — without dragging the whole story around. The moth workshop turns
that into a playful metaphor: a few well-chosen cues on a shelf are enough for a
visitor to be greeted well. No real projects, people, messages, or specifics —
only generic fictional cues and three imaginary moths.

## How to open
Open `index.html` in any modern browser (desktop or phone). No install, no build
step, no server, and no network connection required — everything runs locally in
the one file.

## Controls
- **Tap a cue card** (Step 1) — it flutters to its matching shelf and lights it.
- **Tap a lit shelf** (Step 2) — cycles that shelf's note through a few gentle
  variants, so you can retune what's remembered.
- **Tap a moth visitor** (Step 3) — sends it through; an animated moth threads
  the lit shelves and then carries a line back into the log (Step 4).
- **↺ Reset** — empties every shelf and clears the moths.
- **📋 Copy recipe** — copies a plain-text summary of your shelves and what the
  moths carried, plus the one-line takeaway.
- Fully keyboard/touch friendly: all targets are 44px+ and it respects
  `prefers-reduced-motion` (animations collapse to instant state changes).

## Why it was built
It's today's Daily Surprise for Josh — a small, finished, delightful thing to
poke at for a couple of minutes on a phone. The goal was something tactile and
warm that quietly mirrors the theme of "remember enough to be helpful" without
being a tool, a dashboard, or homework.

## Format choice
- **Lane:** `ai_agent_memory_prototype` — a playful prototype about what a small
  agent/system should keep in memory to behave helpfully on the next visit.
- **Mechanic:** mobile-first spatial matching puzzle / tiny teaching lab. The
  user taps (or drags) glowing memory cue cards into labeled workshop shelves,
  sends tiny moth visitors through, and watches which remembered cue each one
  retrieves — ending with a copyable mini memory recipe. Tactile and playful,
  never a score.

## Avoided recent patterns
Deliberately steered clear of recently used shapes:
- no launch / readiness / shipping simulators
- no scores, grades, pass/fail, or readiness language
- no generic scorecard / rubric / meter
- no abstract AI-agent context / preflight tooling or preflight framing
- no dashboards
- not another card-sort / priority-board mechanic — here each cue *matches* its
  own shelf and the heart of the toy is the moth retrieval, not sorting into
  buckets.

## Privacy check
- No real names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, or locations.
- No dollar amounts, tokens, secrets, or credentials.
- No company / client / project specifics, and no private conversation quotes or
  paraphrases.
- All cue text, moth names, and responses are generic fictional inventions.
- 100% local: no network calls, no external assets, no analytics, no storage of
  anything off-device.
