# Tiny Offer Storyboard

## What it is
A mobile-first, four-panel storyboard editor for turning a fuzzy idea into a crisp narrative
sequence. Each panel is one story beat — **Snag → Shift → Tiny Offer → Next Step**. Tap a panel
to swap its beat framing (each beat cycles through several phrasings + a matching doodle), then
add a tiny editable note to make it yours. When the story feels right, generate a polished,
copyable outline — a one-line story, the four beats, a landing-page outline, and a short
announcement post — all built locally on-device.

It doesn't score, rate, or simulate anything. It just helps you shape a clear little story.

## Privacy-safe inspiration
Recent work energy was about turning noisy project movement into clear, handoff-friendly
sequences. That generic theme — *make the next step obvious, turn scattered movement into a
tidy story* — inspired the snag/shift/offer/step structure. No real project, person, company,
client, message, or specific detail is referenced anywhere.

## How to open
Open `index.html` in any modern browser (desktop or phone). No build step, no install, no
network — it's a single self-contained file. On a phone it's sized for a 390px-wide screen.

## Controls
- **Tap a scene** (the colored beat card) — swaps to the next framing for that beat, with a
  little doodle bounce. The dots show which framing you're on.
- **Note field** — type a concise, specific note (up to 90 chars) under any beat. Your note
  takes priority over the preset framing in the generated outline.
- **✨ Sample** — loads a complete example story so a first-time visitor sees the payoff instantly.
- **↺ Reset** — clears all notes and returns every beat to its first framing.
- **📋 Generate outline** — opens a bottom sheet with your full outline.
- **⧉ Copy** — copies the outline to the clipboard (with a manual-copy fallback).

## Why it was built
A daily creative surprise: a small, useful, playful artifact that helps someone move a vague
idea into a concrete narrative arc — without grading it, gatekeeping it, or pretending to
"simulate a launch." The fun is in tapping through framings and watching a shareable outline
assemble itself.
