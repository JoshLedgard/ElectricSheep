# Thumbprint — A One-Hand Form Rehearsal Lab

*Daily Surprise · 2026-07-31*

## What it is

Thumbprint is a self-contained product-design instrument for discovering how a compact form feels in one hand. Shape a fictional form, perform it inside a phone-like rehearsal surface, then replay the factual interaction trail: field visits, answer edits, backward revisits, pauses, and completion.

Nothing is scored, graded, predicted, or uploaded. The replay preserves what happened chronologically and leaves interpretation to the designer. A harmless fictional studio-hour form is loaded at launch, so the complete build → rehearse → replay → export loop works immediately.

The lab exports a readable JSON interaction receipt and a dependency-free HTML version of the form.

## Privacy-safe inspiration

The generic inspiration was an interest in simplifying how people move through creation forms and making interaction friction tangible. That broad product-design theme became a private rehearsal surface. Thumbprint contains no real people, organizations, projects, accounts, conversations, messages, or datasets.

Anything typed stays only in the open browser tab. There are no accounts, uploads, analytics, network requests, external assets, saved browser data, packages, or server.

## How to open

Open `index.html` in any modern browser. No install, server, account, or network connection is required.

## Controls

1. **Build:** Rename the fictional form, edit field labels, choose text or choice controls, remove fields, or add new ones.
2. **Rehearse:** Complete the form inside the phone surface with a finger, pen, mouse, or keyboard. Jump backward and revise an answer to capture a real revisit.
3. **Replay:** Play the chronological trail. The ribbon shows visits, edits, and the relative pauses between them without turning them into a performance score.
4. **Export:** Download a JSON receipt of the schema and interaction events, or a standalone HTML form prototype.
5. **Rehearse again:** Reset the ephemeral trail and perform another pass.

## Why it was built

Forms are often designed as static stacks, even though people experience them as a sequence of attention, decisions, detours, and revisions. Thumbprint makes that lived sequence visible with no analytics stack and no claim that one path is universally correct. It is a small example of a useful product-design tool that can be dreamed, tried, and carried away in one browser tab.

## Format choice

- **Lane:** `practical_micro_tool` — presented as a believable product-design app / future artifact.
- **Mechanic:** author a compact form → perform an actual one-thumb completion → replay the chronological visit/edit trail → export JSON or standalone HTML. The interaction is direct, local, deterministic, and non-scoring.

This differs from the last three surprises' excavation game, bifocal writing editor, and cyanotype studio. Its form-authoring, live completion, and chronological replay differ from layered excavation, phrase classification/comparison, and compose → expose → rinse.

- No choose-your-own, branching story, microquest, or multiple endings.
- No signal, routing, cartography, constellation, or path-connection metaphor.
- No launch, readiness, shipping, or preflight simulator.
- No scorecard, rubric meter, grade, benchmark, or generic generator.
- No abstract AI-agent context or debugging tool.
- No family/travel planner, sports-data toy, or local/home utility.

## Privacy check

- The built-in content is fictional and generic.
- No real names, companies, email addresses, locations, financial details, account data, or conversation excerpts are present.
- User-entered text is escaped before display and remains within the current tab.
- Export occurs only when the user deliberately downloads a local file.
