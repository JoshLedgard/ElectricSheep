# Breakpoint Beat Lab

## What it is

Breakpoint Beat Lab is a self-contained responsive-layout instrument. Five draggable interface blocks double as musical tracks. Move them around, resize the simulated browser from desktop to phone, and press play: the spatial arrangement becomes a looping beat.

No server, account, package installation, network connection, or external library is required.

## Privacy-safe inspiration

The broad inspiration was the craft of understanding how interfaces behave at different sizes and making web experiences feel useful. It deliberately turns that generic product-design energy into an expressive instrument rather than reproducing any real page, data, conversation, customer, or project.

## How to open

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. Audio is muted until **Play layout** is pressed and gracefully remains visual if Web Audio is unavailable.

## Controls

- **Drag a colored block:** changes where its musical event lands and, for some tracks, its pitch or depth.
- **Viewport slider:** moves between wide, compact, and phone layouts; blocks reflow at responsive breakpoints.
- **Play layout / Stop:** starts or stops the sixteen-step scan head.
- **Remix:** creates a fresh spatial composition.
- **Reset:** restores the original arrangement and viewport.

All primary controls meet a 44px minimum touch-target goal, and block dragging uses Pointer Events for touch, mouse, and pen.

## Why it was built

Responsive design is normally inspected with rulers and breakpoints. This experiment asks whether it can be *heard*. It is a small fake app that makes the relationship between spatial hierarchy, reflow, repetition, and rhythm tangible in two minutes.

## Format choice

- **Lane:** Creative coding instrument / product prototype
- **Mechanic:** Direct manipulation of draggable responsive-layout blocks plus a looping scan-head that turns spatial composition and breakpoint reflow into rhythm and sound

This differs from the previous three surprises' tactile timeline, product-archaeology deduction, and one-button physics mechanics.
