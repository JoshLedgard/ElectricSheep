# Changelog Noir

## What it is

A compact product-archaeology game: compare three fictional app interfaces before and after, tap the three meaningful changes in each case, then infer the product decision behind the revision. It is linear, deterministic, self-contained, and designed for a satisfying two-to-three-minute play session.

## Privacy-safe inspiration

The inspiration is the general craft of reviewing product changes and turning operational evidence into clear decisions. All products, people, copy, and situations in the game are fictional.

## How to open

Open `index.html` in any modern browser. It needs no server, package installation, account, or network connection.

## Controls

- Select **Open Case 01** to begin.
- Tap changed interface regions in either the Before or After phone.
- Use **Hint** if a clue is hard to spot.
- After pinning three clues, choose the product motive that best explains the whole revision.
- Complete all three cases, then use **Reopen Cases** to replay.
- Sound feedback is optional and begins only after interaction. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.

## Why it was built

Most spot-the-difference games stop at visual detection. Changelog Noir adds a second layer: interpreting product intent. It turns interface review into a small piece of design reasoning with a premium noir presentation.

## Format choice

- **Lane:** `game_or_puzzle`
- **Mechanic:** `side-by-side interface diffing + tap evidence + product-intent inference`

## Explicit bans avoided

This is not a field guide, atlas, almanac, codex, bestiary, zine, or pocket guide. It has no choose-your-own, branching-adventure, microquest, or multiple-ending structure. It uses no signal, cartographer, constellation, routing, or path-connection metaphor. It is not a launch/readiness/shipping simulator, scorecard, rubric meter, AI-agent context/preflight/debugger, or generic generator. It contains no family, travel, baseball, home, or Seattle utility.

## Privacy

The artifact contains no real names, companies, projects, emails, phone numbers, addresses, financial data, exact conversation excerpts, or personal details.
