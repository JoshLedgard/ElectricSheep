# Mixshift

An offline, mobile-first interactive data experiment about weighted averages and Simpson's paradox. Synthetic help-desk, search, and learning scenarios let you change each option's share of easy cases, compare within-cohort rates to the overall weighted result, and see when the headline reverses.

**Inspiration:** The general idea that purpose-built interfaces can help people notice what a single aggregate number obscures; no personal or private context is represented.

**Open:** Open `index.html` in any modern browser, or use the published link. No server, account, packages, or network required.

**Controls:** Choose a scenario; drag or keyboard-adjust the two easy-case-share sliders. “Equalize the mix” aligns B's share with A's to remove mix imbalance; “Restore preset” resets both; “Copy finding” copies a plain-text explanation when browser clipboard permission allows it.

**Why:** When different cohorts are distributed unevenly, a combined metric can say the opposite of every segment. Interacting with the traffic mix makes that caveat tangible rather than merely stated.

**Format choice:** lane: `data_visualization`; mechanic: `drag cohort-mix sliders to compare fixed segment rates against recalculated weighted overall bars`.

**Explicit bans avoided:** No field guide/zine/atlas framing, choose-your-own or branching story, signal routing/cartography/constellation, launch or readiness simulator, scorecard/rubric meter, abstract agent preflight, or puzzle mechanic from the recent builds. No names, emails, addresses, real financial amounts, conversation quotes, private data, or analytics.
