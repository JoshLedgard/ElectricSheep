# Pixel Witness

## What it is

A three-round change-blindness game disguised as a set of polished fictional app interfaces. Each scene flickers between two versions with a brief visual mask; find and tap the five details that changed before your visual memory talks you out of them.

## Privacy-safe inspiration

This was inspired by a generic product-design question: after a substantial web interface changes, what do people actually notice? The game turns that question into a compact perception experiment. It contains no real product, company, conversation, or personal information.

## How to open

Open `index.html` in any modern browser. It is self-contained and requires no server, installation, account, or network connection.

## Controls

- Press **Start observing** to begin.
- Tap or click any detail that changes between the two versions.
- Use **Pause flicker** to freeze the current version.
- Press and hold **Hold a hint** to briefly reveal the neighborhood of one unfound change.
- Toggle the **♪** button for optional synthesized sound; audio never autoplays.
- On a keyboard, **Space** pauses or resumes and holding **H** shows a hint.
- With the system's Reduce Motion preference enabled, automatic flicker is replaced by a manual **Show other version** control.
- Finish all three scenes to see elapsed time, tap accuracy, and the locally stored best time. **Play again** resets the run.

## Why it was built

Pixel Witness is a tiny playable reminder that noticing is an interaction, not a guarantee. It aims to feel complete in two or three minutes while showing off accurate canvas hit-testing, responsive touch controls, handcrafted fictional UI, accessible motion preferences, and optional browser-generated audio in one offline file.
